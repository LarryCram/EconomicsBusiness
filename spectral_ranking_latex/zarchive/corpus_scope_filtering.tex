\subsection{Cross-check against the OpenAlex Discipline-Scoped Journal List}
\label{sec:ojl_crosscheck}

The 2{,}751 long-list OAS are compiled from disciplinary registers,
each of which casts a deliberately broad net:
the ERA SJL encompasses any journal with a business or economics secondary classification,
the MJL includes transportation and management science outlets,
and Harzing's JQL includes journals that, while valuable references for management
researchers, sit primarily in adjacent social-science or natural-science disciplines.
To assess whether each long-list entry has substantive disciplinary coverage,
the long-list is cross-checked against a second OpenAlex-derived resource---here
called the \textit{OpenAlex Journal List} (OJL).

The OJL is constructed from the OpenAlex topics taxonomy.
Each source record in the OpenAlex snapshot carries a list of topic assignments derived
from the content of its indexed works, with each topic linked to a hierarchical
\textit{subfield}, \textit{field}, and \textit{domain}.
The OJL retains all OpenAlex sources for which at least one indexed work carries a
topic belonging to Field~14 (\textit{Economics, Econometrics and Finance}) or
Field~20 (\textit{Business and Management}).
This yields 42{,}756 distinct source entities.
For each such source, the OJL records the \textit{in-scope ratio}---the share of
the source's indexed works whose primary topic falls within Fields~14 or~20---as an
indicator of disciplinary concentration.

\subsubsection*{Long-list OAS matched to OJL}

Matching is performed by OA source identifier and, where identifiers do not agree
between the two snapshots, by shared ISSN (using the OpenAlex preferred ISSN
\texttt{issn\_l} and all alternative ISSNs on both sides).
Of the 2{,}751 long-list OAS, 2{,}289 (83.2\%) are found in the OJL,
confirming that a substantial majority of the register-derived list is independently
recognised by OpenAlex as having economics or business content.
The remaining 462 OAS (16.8\%) are absent from the OJL, meaning that OpenAlex
has not classified any of their indexed works under Fields~14 or~20.

\subsubsection*{OJL sources not in the long-list}

Conversely, of the 42{,}756 OJL sources, only 2{,}289 (5.4\%) appear in the
disciplinary register long-list.
This large residual reflects the heterogeneous composition of OJL:
it includes every source for which even a single work is classified in
Fields~14 or~20, irrespective of that source's primary disciplinary focus.
Restricting to sources with in-scope ratio above 0.40 yields 9{,}624 OJL sources
absent from the long-list.

These high-ratio OJL sources were examined to understand the gap.
The 5{,}341 OJL sources with in-scope ratio above 0.60 that do not appear in the
long-list are categorised in Table~\ref{tab:ojl_unmatched}.
The dominant categories are low-output sources (28.6\%; fewer than 30 indexed
works), journals not represented in any of the three disciplinary registers
(27.1\%), and non-Anglophone or regional journals unlikely to be captured by the
primarily English-language registers (21.8\%).
A further 12.7\% carry no ISSN in OpenAlex and are therefore unmatchable regardless
of register coverage.
Together these structural reasons account for over 90\% of the gap.
No further sources are added to the long-list on the basis of the OJL cross-check,
as the residual journals are either low in aggregate output, absent from established
disciplinary rankings, or lack the identifiers required for reliable provenance tracking.

\subsection{Scope Filtering of the Long-list OAS}
\label{sec:scope_filter}

The 462 long-list OAS absent from the OJL are examined to assess whether their
absence reflects a genuine disciplinary mismatch or a structural gap in OpenAlex's
topic coverage.
By construction, absence from the OJL implies that OpenAlex has not assigned any
work from that source to Fields~14 or~20.
This can arise for two substantively different reasons.

First, a journal may have been captured by the disciplinary registers but is
primarily housed in an adjacent field---psychology, political science, statistics,
or the natural sciences---and appears in the register only because of a secondary
or borderline classification.
Examples include \textit{Nature Communications}, \textit{American Psychological
Review}, and \textit{American Political Science Review}, all of which appear in
Harzing's JQL as reference journals for management researchers but whose indexed
content is overwhelmingly outside the target fields.

Second, the OpenAlex topic classifier may not have assigned Fields~14 or~20 to the
works of a genuine economics or management journal.
This occurs most visibly for the 82 long-list OAS that carry a MJL classification
in \textsc{Economics}, \textsc{Transportation}, \textsc{Management},
\textsc{Business}, or \textsc{Business, Finance}: these sources are confirmed
in-scope by the Web of Science category system, yet are absent from the OJL
because OpenAlex classifies their content primarily under transport science,
operations research, or adjacent technology fields rather than Fields~14 or~20.
Notable examples include \textit{Transportation Research} parts B, D, and F,
\textit{Journal of Transport Geography}, and
\textit{Accident Analysis and Prevention}.

Each of the 462 OAS absent from OJL is assigned a scope signal
based on register membership and journal name (Table~\ref{tab:scope_signals}).

\subsubsection*{Activity and output criteria}

Two further criteria are applied uniformly across all 2{,}751 long-list OAS,
drawing on publication-year data from the OJL for the 2{,}289 matched sources.
A source is flagged \textit{Inactive pre-2000} if its last indexed publication
year in OpenAlex is before 2000; such sources cannot contribute works to a
modern research corpus.
A source is flagged \textit{Very low output} if the ratio of its indexed-work
count to the length of its active period (last minus first publication year plus
one) falls below 1.0, indicating negligible OpenAlex coverage regardless of
whether the journal itself is active.
This criterion reflects the practical constraint that work extraction is from
OpenAlex: a source with fewer than one indexed work per year will contribute
negligibly to the corpus, whether because the journal is genuinely thin or because
its content is predominantly behind a paywall and therefore unindexed.

The 2{,}341 in-scope OAS form the \textit{scope-filtered source list} used for
work extraction.
Of these, 2{,}259 are confirmed in-scope by the OJL cross-check
(in-scope ratio~$>0$), and 82 are retained on the strength of their Web of Science
category despite the absence of a corresponding OJL record.
The 33 JQL$+$SJL borderline sources are included in the count pending manual review;
reclassification of any of these as out-of-scope would reduce the list proportionally.
The 25 very-low-output sources represent a practical rather than a disciplinary
exclusion: their OpenAlex coverage is too sparse to contribute meaningfully
to a works-based analysis, but their disciplinary classification is not in question.
