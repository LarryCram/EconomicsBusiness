% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Journal--institution citation networks}
\label{sec:networks}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

We begin by selecting $N_s$ sources $\mathfrak{S}$ in a set of fields, disciplines or categories $\mathfrak{F}$.
Then we select $N_w$ works $\mathfrak{W}$ published in $\mathfrak{S}$ within the census
window years $t_c = [t^c_0 \ldots t^c_1]$ or target years $t_t = [t^t_0 \ldots t^t_1]$.
From the reference lists of the set of census works $\mathfrak{W}^c$ we identify the set
of target works $\mathfrak{W}^t$ and form the filtered reference set 
$\mathfrak{R}=\{r(i,j):w_i\in\mathfrak{W}^c,\,w_j\in\mathfrak{W}^t\}$.
Author affiliations in $\mathfrak{W}$ establish a set of institutions $\mathfrak{U}'$ and 
authors $\mathfrak{A}'$. In practice many institutions (and sources) publishing only 
a few works per year can be dropped from the corpus. Let $\bar{w}_u$ be the mean annual 
census-window work count for institution $u$ and $\bar{w}_s$ the corresponding work count for source $s$. We determine lower limits $\tau_U$ and $\tau_S$ by examining the respective frequency distributions and define
$\mathfrak{U}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}'$ as the institutions with $\bar{w}_u\geq\tau_U$,
and $\mathfrak{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}'$ as the sources with $\bar{w}_s\geq\tau_S$,
with corresponding author set $\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}'$.
Works that lose all their authorships under the institution filter, or whose source is
dropped by the source filter, are removed from the corpus.

We say that a work $w_i$ in $\mathfrak{W}^c$ 
transfers (or pays) \textit{attention} to its references $r_{ij}$. Let $R_i=|\{j:(i,j)\in\mathfrak{R}\}|$ be the reference count of work
$w_i$ and $\bar{R}=|\mathfrak{R}|/N_w$ the mean reference count over
$\mathfrak{W}$. Since reference lists have different lengths,
we consider two cases for the transferred attention per reference,

\begin{equation}
	\rho_i \;=\;
	\begin{cases}
		 1 & \text{(full reference counting)} \\
		\bar{R}/R_i & \text{(fractional reference counting).}
	\end{cases}
	\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Under full reference counting a work with twice as many references transfers twice
as much attention; under fractional reference counting every work transfers the
same total attention $\bar{R}$ \citep{zittModifyingJournalImpact2008}. 

A work's attention can be conferred on or allocated to its retained source and institutions. The corpus parameter $\chi$ sets the division of conferred attention between the source $(\chi)$ and institutions $(1-\chi)$. While at first sight the choice $\chi=0.5$ appears sound,
we see below that $\chi=N_S/(N_S+N_I)$ arises naturally once all works have been accumulated. 
We consider two models for conferral of a work's attention on its institutions (i.e. the \textit{institution weight})

\begin{equation}
  \omega_{i u} \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\sum_{\ell:\,u\in\mathcal{u}_{i\ell}}
  \frac{1}{a_i}\,\frac{1}{u_{i\ell}}, & \text{(author-fractional)} \\[10pt]
  \dfrac{1}{n_i}, & \text{(institution-fractional)}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
where $n_i = |\bigcup_\ell \mathcal{u}_{i\ell}|$ is the number of institutions of work $w_i$, and $\ell$ indexes the authors of work $w_i$, $a_i$ is their count, and $u_{i\ell}$ the number of institutions of author $\ell$, so that $\sum_u\omega_{iu}=1$ under author-fractional counting \citep{waltmanFieldnormalizedCitationImpact2015}. The decision to re-weight fractional counts after any institution pruning is a trade-off between boosting a work's remaining institutions and diminishing the work's total contribution. 

We construct the set of \emph{units} [sources or institutions;
\cite{pinskiCitationInfluenceJournal1976}]
$\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{S}\ \cup\ \mathfrak{U}$ with $N=N_s+N_u$ elements,
ordered so that sources occupy indices $1,\dots,N_s$ and institutions
indices $N_s+1,\dots,N$. Each reference $r(i,j) \in \mathfrak{R}$ carries attention from the
units associated with $w_i$ to the units associated with $w_j$,
across four unit-type pairs: source--source ($SS$), source--institution
($SI$), institution--source ($IS$), and institution--institution ($II$).

For each work $w_i$ we define the source membership one-hot vector
$\mathbf{b}^S_i\in\mathbb{R}^{N_s}$ with $[\mathbf{b}^S_i]_s=\mathbf{1}[w_i\in s]$,
and the institution weight distribution vector
$\mathbf{b}^U_i\in\mathbb{R}^{N_u}$ with $[\mathbf{b}^U_i]_u=\omega_{iu}$
from \eqref{eq:omega}. The attention carried by each reference is distributed across unit
pairs and accumulated as four block matrices over $\mathfrak{R}$ via
rank-1 outer products,
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf{C}_{XY} \;=\; \sum_{(i,j)\,\in\,\mathfrak{R}}
	\rho_i\;\mathbf{b}^X_i\,\bigl(\mathbf{b}^Y_j\bigr)^\top,
	\qquad XY\in\{SS,\,SI,\,IS,\,II\},
	\label{eq:Cblock}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{b}^X_i$ distributes attention from the citing side across
units of type $X$, and $\mathbf{b}^Y_j$ distributes it to the cited side
across units of type $Y$.
Each entry $[\mathbf{C}_{XY}]_{pq}$ is therefore proportional to the
$\rho_i$-weighted attention flowing from unit $p$ of type $X$ to unit
$q$ of type $Y$ across all references in $\mathfrak{R}$.

The citation matrix assembles the four blocks:
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf{C}(\chi,\mathbf{m},\rho) \;=\;
	\begin{bmatrix}
		m_{SS}(1-\chi)^2\,\mathbf{C}_{SS} &
		m_{SI}\,\chi(1-\chi)\,\mathbf{C}_{SI} \\[4pt]
		m_{IS}\,\chi(1-\chi)\,\mathbf{C}_{IS} &
		m_{II}\,\chi^2\,\mathbf{C}_{II}
	\end{bmatrix},
	\label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
where the binary \textit{block mask} $\mathbf{m}=(m_{SS},m_{SI},m_{IS},m_{II})\in\{0,1\}^4$
is bibliometrically meaningful for
source-only $(1000)$, institution-only $(0001)$,
bipartite $(0110)$, and full-joint $(1111)$ combined blocks.
The mixing weight $\chi$ controls the source--institution balance
in the bipartite and full-joint cases and is inert in the single-mode cases.
The $\chi$ scaling preserves total attention flow across all block combinations,
since $(1-\chi)^2+2\chi(1-\chi)+\chi^2=1$.

Note that the corpus excludes (a) census work references not giving
attention to target works, (b) non-census works giving attention to
target works, and (c) authorships that include an omitted institution.

\paragraph{Ordinal spectral ranking.}

The attention of a unit is amplified
or diminished by the attention that it receives from the units connected to it via the citation matrix. This leads to an eigenvector equation in which the attention
of each unit is defined recursively as the weighted sum of the attention
of the units that reference it, with its value established by the
convergence of that recursion.
The process of finding the fixed point is \textit{spectral ranking}
(a verb); the resulting vector is the \textit{spectral ranking} (a noun).
The vector can be read from either side of the transaction --- from the
referencing side as attention, or from the cited side as influence --- just
as $\mathbf{C}$ can be called a reference matrix or a citation matrix
depending on which side one stands.

Let $r_p=\sum_q C_{pq}$ be the sum of fractionated references given by unit $p$
and $\mathbf{D}_r=\mathrm{diag}(r_p)$ the corresponding diagonal matrix.
Row-normalising $\mathbf{C}$ to obtain $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{D}_r^{-1}\mathbf{C}$
ensures that each unit's fractionated references are equally valued regardless of how
many references it makes: a unit with many references exerts no more
attention per reference than one with few (this consideration obtains when $\rho_i = 1$).
Reference intensity variations are removed at this stage and reintroduced
only at the end via the influence per work scaling \eqref{eq:vstar}.
If $r_p=0$ (a dangling unit with no outgoing references) the corresponding
row of $\mathbf{H}$ is defined to be zero.

The three unipartite masks---source-only $(1000)$, institution-only $(0001)$,
and full-joint $(1111)$---each yield a square $\mathbf{H}$ to which the
following spectral ranking theory applies directly.
The spectral ranking $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$
\citep[see][for an overview]{vignaSpectralRanking2016}
is the unique positive solution to the eigenvector equation,
\begin{equation}
    (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{H}^\top)\boldsymbol{\pi}^* = \mathbf{0},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{1}^\top\boldsymbol{\pi}^*=1,
    \quad
    \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\ge 0,
    \label{eq:eig}
\end{equation}
if $\mathbf{H}$ is primitive (irreducible and aperiodic), as guaranteed
by the Perron--Frobenius theorem. Our numerical implementation tests primitivity
for every $\mathbf{H}$ as explained below and has not encountered an imprimitive case.
\cite{gellerCitationInfluenceMethodology1978} showed that
$\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$ is the stationary distribution of the
Markov chain with transition matrix $\mathbf{H}$, and that it coincides
with the \textit{influence weight} of \cite{pinskiCitationInfluenceJournal1976}
scaled by the reference count proportion $r_p / \sum_q r_q$.

\cite{katzNewStatusIndex1953} proposed attenuating attention along walks
of length $k$ by a factor $\alpha^k$, $\alpha\in(0,1)$, so that distant
attention counts for less than proximate ones.
Summing attenuated walks of all lengths and endowing every unit with a
uniform initial score of $1$ yields the Katz resolvent equation
\[
    (\mathbf{I} - \alpha\mathbf{H}^\top)\boldsymbol{\pi} = \mathbf{1}.
\]
Since $\mathbf{H}$ is row-stochastic, $(\mathbf{I}-\alpha\mathbf{H}^\top)$
is invertible for any $\alpha\in(0,1)$.

\cite{hubbellInputoutputApproachClique1965} generalised this by replacing
the uniform endowment $\mathbf{1}$ with an informative prior
$\boldsymbol{\mu}$ over units, representing exogenous information about
standing independent of citation structure,
\[
    (\mathbf{I} - \alpha\mathbf{H}^\top)\boldsymbol{\pi} = \boldsymbol{\mu}.
\]
We use the uniform prior $\mu_p = 1/N_{\mathfrak{P}}$ for $p\in\mathfrak{P}$.
Note that the intermediate case $(\mathbf{I}-\alpha\mathbf{H}^\top)\boldsymbol{\pi}=\mathbf{0}$ with $\alpha\ne 1$ admits only the trivial 
solution $\boldsymbol{\pi}=\mathbf{0}$ and is not useful as a ranking.

Combining attention attenuation and prior information yields the general
resolvent equation
\begin{equation}
    (\mathbf{I} - \alpha\mathbf{H}^\top)\boldsymbol{\pi}
    \;=\;
    (1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mu},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{1}^\top\boldsymbol{\pi}=1,
    \quad
    \boldsymbol{\pi}\ge 0,
    \label{eq:spectral}
\end{equation}
which recovers $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$ in the limit $\alpha\to 1$ when
$\mathbf{H}$ is primitive.
While $\alpha$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ are introduced here for their
substantive interpretations---attention attenuation and exogenous prior
respectively---they have the additional effect of ensuring a unique
solution to~\eqref{eq:spectral} even when $\mathbf{H}$ is not primitive:
$\alpha<1$ makes $(\mathbf{I}-\alpha\mathbf{H}^\top)$ invertible, and a
nonzero $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ lifts the equation off the homogeneous system. If $\mathbf{C}$ were a binary matrix, this would be the PageRank spectral ranking \citep{page}

The spectral ranking $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ reflects the total attention or influence of each unit and depends on unit size.
The \emph{influence per work} \citep{palacios-huertaMeasurementIntellectualInfluence2004},
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}
    \;=\;
    A\,\mathbf{A}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\pi},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{A}=\mathrm{diag}(a_p),
    \qquad
    A = \mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{1} = \textstyle\sum_p a_p,
    \label{eq:vstar}
\end{equation}
removes this dependence, where the unit work count $a_p = |\{i:w_i\in p\}|$ where $p$ is a specific source or institution,
and $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ denotes the solution to \eqref{eq:spectral}.
The scalar $A$ normalises $\mathbf{v}$ so that its $a_p$-weighted mean equals one:
$\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{v}/A=\mathbf{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\pi}=1$.
A unit with $v_p=1$ therefore has the corpus-average influence per work and
$v_p=2$ indicates twice the average influence per work.
\cite{palacios-huertaMeasurementIntellectualInfluence2004} show that
$\mathbf{v}$ is uniquely characterised by several reasonable axiomatic
constraints \citep[but see][]{serranoMeasurementIntellectualInfluence2004}.

\paragraph{Bipartite ranking.}
With $\mathbf{m}=(0110)$, equation~\eqref{eq:C} yields block off-diagonal matrices $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{H}$,
forming a directed bipartite network between sources~($S$) and institutions~($I$).
This is the mask used as the baseline model in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
Partitioning rows and columns into the $S$ and $I$ blocks, the stochastic matrix takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{H}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{H}_{SI} \\ \mathbf{H}_{IS} & \mathbf{0} \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:H_bipartite}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{H}_{SI}$ distributes a source's attention across institutions and
$\mathbf{H}_{IS}$ does the reverse.
Because every path alternates $S\to I\to S\to\cdots$, this structure makes $\mathbf{H}$
periodic with period~2.

Squaring $\mathbf{H}$ collapses two-step walks to separate node types, giving one-mode relations
\[
  \mathbf{H}^2
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{M}_S & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{M}_I \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{M}_S = \mathbf{H}_{SI}\mathbf{H}_{IS},
  \quad
  \mathbf{M}_I = \mathbf{H}_{IS}\mathbf{H}_{SI}.
\]
Both $\mathbf{M}_S$ and $\mathbf{M}_I$ are row-stochastic products of row-stochastic matrices.

To make the bipartite mode comparable with the unipartite SS and II modes at the same $\alpha$, single steps $SI$ or $IS$ should be damped by $\sqrt{\alpha}$, so the round-trip incurs attenuation $\sqrt{\alpha}\cdot\sqrt{\alpha}=\alpha$. 
Substituting $\sqrt{\alpha}$ for $\alpha$ in equation~\eqref{eq:eig},
partitioning $\boldsymbol{\pi}=(\boldsymbol{\pi}_S,\boldsymbol{\pi}_I)$,
and eliminating $\boldsymbol{\pi}_I$ yields the bipartite resolvent for sources,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\mathbf{I}-\alpha\mathbf{M}_S^\top\bigr)\boldsymbol{\pi}_S
  \;=\;
  (1-\sqrt{\alpha})\bigl(\boldsymbol{\mu}_S+\sqrt{\alpha}\,\mathbf{H}_{IS}^\top\boldsymbol{\mu}_I\bigr),
  \label{eq:kh_source}
\end{equation}
Note that when $\alpha < 1, \mu > 0$, the right-hand side links institutions to sources via $\mathbf{H}_{IS}^\top$.  Institution ranking can be computed from source ranking by the matrix--vector product,
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\pi}_I
  \;=\;
  \sqrt{\alpha}\,\mathbf{H}_{SI}^\top\boldsymbol{\pi}_S+(1-\sqrt{\alpha})\boldsymbol{\mu}_I.
  \label{eq:kh_inst}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Computation.}
Defining the modified matrix
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf{\tilde{H}}
	\;=\;
	\alpha\mathbf{H}+(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mu}\mathbf{1}^\top,
	\label{eq:Hstar}
\end{equation} 
the resolvent
$\boldsymbol{\pi}=\mathbf{\tilde{H}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\pi}$
is equivalent to equation~\eqref{eq:spectral}. This is the general form of 
our numerical problem, although most of the results reported below use $\alpha = 1$ and $\mu = 0$ so $\mathbf{\tilde{H}} = \mathbf{H}$. 

As explained below, we construct many realisations of $\mathbf{\tilde{H}}$ while testing different models for unit pairings, subfields and other parameters, and to run our bootstrap investigations. These realisations correspond to a unit-unit citation network with one giant strongly connected component (SCC) and some very small, usually singleton, components that are sources or institutions disjoint with the particular network under investigation. In these cases we retain only the SCC. For some units in the SCC, there may be no outgoing references, and for others, no incoming references. We exclude these units too, ensuring that the residual $\mathbf{\tilde{H}}$ is highly likely to be row-stochastic and primitive, with a single dominate eigenvector.

$\mathbf{\tilde{H}}$ is a sparse matrix constructed from the respective filtered and weighted edge list $\mathcal{E}$ accumulated over $\mathfrak{R}$ and stored in compressed sparse row (CSR) format. We have explored two approaches to applying the resolvent. The Python scipy function \textit{scipy.sparse.linalg.eigs} efficiently provides all eigenpairs, allowing us to review the primitivity condition. The power iteration 
\begin{equation}
	\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(k+1)}
	\;=\;
	\mathbf{\tilde{H}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(k)},
	\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
converges to $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ from any strictly positive starting
vector $\boldsymbol{\pi}^{(0)}$. In either approach, the per-work scaling equation~\eqref{eq:vstar} is applied after $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ has been computed.
