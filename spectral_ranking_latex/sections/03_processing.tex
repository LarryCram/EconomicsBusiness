\section{Corpus selection}
\label{sec:sources}

OpenAlex \citep{priemOpenAlexFullyopenIndex2022} provides our bibliometric data. Corpus selection starts with a list of eligible sources having three features: evidence from one of our journal registries that the source
publishes economics or business research, an OpenAlex source identifier for corpus extraction, and a sufficient number of published works per year.

The journal registries are as follows. Harzing's \textit{71st Edition of the Journal Quality List}~\cite[][JQL]{harzingJournalQualityList2024} assembles
peer assessments of journal quality across business and economics
disciplines. Its scope is confined to economics and business so no
filtering is applied.
The \textit{ERA 2023 Submission Journal List}~\cite[][SJL]{australianresearchcouncilERA2023Submission2022} assigns up to three
Field of Research (FoR) codes to each entry.
From the full list of 26{,}241 journals we retain those for which \emph{every} assigned FoR code falls within
Division~35 (Commerce, Management, Tourism and Services) or
Division~38 (Economics)---specifically the four-digit groups
3501--3509 and 3801--3803, 3899.
\textit{Clarivate Web of Science Journal Citation Reports}~\cite[][JCR]{clarivateanalyticsJournalCitationReports2026} assign each
journal a Web of Science category. We retain those in
``Economics'', ``Management'', ``Business'', ``Transportation'',
or ``Business, Finance''.

Every journal in JQL, SJL, and JCR has at least one ISSN, and may have several. These registry ISSNs are joined independently to the February 2026 OpenAlex sources snapshot using its list of ISSNs as the matching key and selecting only the `journal' source-type. Around 30 additional matches are identified by exact title matching for OpenAlex sources that lack an ISSN. We denote the union of matched OpenAlex sources OAS\textsuperscript{*}.

For OAS\textsuperscript{*} sources we extract work and authorship data and examine the annual-output distributions for sources and institutions. Retention curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:retention}) show that a majority of institutions and a substantial fraction of sources contributing fewer than 20 works per year in the census window, producing long tails that contribute little stable citation density. We therefore adopt the thresholds $\tau_S = 20$ works/source/year and $\tau_U = 20$ works/institution/year. Sources retained under $\tau_S$ form the set OAS; institutions retained under $\tau_U$ form $\mathfrak{U}$.

Using sources and institutions that satisfy these thresholds, we extract works, authorships and references published between 2000 and 2024, retaining works typed as `article' or `review' and excluding works that are paratext, retracted, or without an authorship entity. For the baseline results of Section~\ref{sec:results} the census and target windows coincide at $t^c = t^t = [2020, 2024]$; the full 2000--2024 extraction supports the time-series sensitivity runs $t_1,\dots,t_4$ reported there.

Sources span subfields $F$ within the broad $\mathfrak{F}$ set of sources in Economics and Business, labelled as follows. Each source in OAS carries one or more subfield labels designating categories, groups, subjects, and topics in JQL, SJR, JCR, and OAJ respectively. These labels are mapped by hand to the broad subfields $E$ (economics) and $B$ (business), or left unmapped (e.g. Psychology, Education from JQL). Ambiguous labels (e.g. Finance, Accounting) are mapped to $E$. The mappings are counted, and the source classified as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
	\item `E' when its E-count is $\geq 2$ and its B-count is $< 2$ (economics-dominant);
	\item `B' when its B-count is $\geq 2$ and its E-count is $< 2$ (business-dominant);
	\item `A' when both E-count and B-count are $\geq 2$ (genuinely ambiguous);
	\item `X' when both E-count and B-count are $< 2$ (weak signal in both fields).
\end{itemize}
Institutions generally publish in multiple subfields, so direct categorical
classification is unsuitable. Instead, we characterise each institution by
the stochastic reference matrix $H_{SI}$. For each source~$s$,
$H_{SI}[s,U]$ is the fraction of $s$'s citation weight directed at
institution~$u$, so every source contributes equally regardless of its total
output. The field-$F$ score for institution~$u$ is
$\sum_{k:\,\mathrm{subfield}(k)=F} H_{SI}[k,u]$.
An institution is assigned $F = X$ if its $X$-score is at least
$\tfrac{1}{2}$; otherwise it takes the dominant label among $\{E, B, A\}$.
