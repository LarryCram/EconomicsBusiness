\section{Corpus selection}
\label{sec:sources}

OpenAlex \citep{priemOpenAlexFullyopenIndex2022, alperinAnalysisSuitabilityOpenAlex2024} provides our bibliometric data. Corpus selection starts with a list of eligible sources having three features: evidence from one of our journal registries that the source
publishes economics or business research, an OpenAlex source identifier for corpus extraction, and a sufficient number of published works per year.

The journal registries are as follows. Harzing's \textit{71st Edition of the Journal Quality List}~\cite[][JQL]{harzingJournalQualityList2024} assembles
peer assessments of journal quality across business and economics
disciplines. Its scope is confined to economics and business so no
filtering is applied.
The \textit{ERA 2023 Submission Journal List}~\cite[][SJL]{australianresearchcouncilERA2023Submission2022} assigns up to three
Field of Research (FoR) codes to each entry.
From the full list of 26{,}241 journals we retain those for which \emph{every} assigned FoR code falls within
Division~35 (Commerce, Management, Tourism and Services) or
Division~38 (Economics)---specifically the 35 and 38 two-digit divisions and the four-digit groups
3501--3509 and 3801--3803, 3899.
\textit{Clarivate Web of Science Journal Citation Reports}~\cite[][JCR]{clarivateanalyticsJournalCitationReports2026} assign each
journal a Web of Science category. We retain those in
``Economics'', ``Management'', ``Business'', ``Transportation'',
or ``Business, Finance''.
The \textit{Scopus Source List}~\cite[][SSL]{elsevierScopusSourceList2026} (March 2026 edition) assigns All Science Journal Classification (ASJC) codes to each source. We retain journals carrying code~1400 (Business, Management and Accounting) or code~2000 (Economics, Econometrics and Finance), including inactive titles.

Every journal in JQL, SJL, JCR, and SSL has at least one ISSN, and may have several. These registry ISSNs are joined independently to the February 2026 OpenAlex sources snapshot using its list of ISSNs as the matching key and selecting only the `journal' source-type. Around 30 additional matches are identified by exact title matching for OpenAlex sources that lack an ISSN. We denote the union of matched OpenAlex sources OAS\textsuperscript{*}.

For OAS\textsuperscript{*} sources we extract work and authorship data and examine the annual-output distributions for sources and institutions. The retention curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:retention}) plot the decline in unit count as the thresholds ($\tau_S,~\tau_I$) for works published per year rises, revealing that a majority of institutions and a substantial fraction of sources contributes fewer than 20 works per year in the census window. The long tails add negligible amounts to the citation density, and accordingly we adopt thresholds $\tau_S = 20$ works/source/year and $\tau_I = 20$ works/institution/year. Sources retained above the threshold $\tau_S$ form the set OAS, and institutions retained under $\tau_I$ form $\mathfrak{I}$.

Using sources and institutions that satisfy these thresholds, we extract works, authorships and references published between 2000 and 2024, retaining works typed as `article' or `review' and excluding works that are paratext, retracted, or without an authorship entity. Although most results relate to the recent 5-year window 2020-2024, the wider 2000--2024 time window supports a time-series analysis.

Within the broad field of Economics and Business, many sources publish works in subfields $F$ within the set $\mathfrak{F}$. To relate OAS sources to subfields ${F}$ we capitalise on registry subfield labels (categories, groups, subjects, and topics in JQL, SJR, JCR, and OAS respectively). These labels are mapped by hand to the broad subfields $F=E$ (economics) or $F=B$ (business), or $F=X$ when the subfield is out of scope (e.g. Psychology, Education from JQL). Fields in Finance and in Accounting are mapped to $F=E$. The SSL ASJC codes contribute directly: code~2000 increments the E-count and code~1400 increments the B-count. Some sources publish sizeable amounts in both E and B; they are designated ambiguous, $F=A$. The five signals are counted, and each source classified as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
	\item `E' when its E-count is $\geq 3$ and its B-count is $< 3$ (economics-dominant);
	\item `B' when its B-count is $\geq 3$ and its E-count is $< 3$ (business-dominant);
	\item `A' when both E-count and B-count are $\geq 3$ (genuinely ambiguous);
	\item `X' when both E-count and B-count are $< 3$ (weak signal in both fields).
\end{itemize}
Institutions generally publish in multiple subfields, so direct categorical
classification is unsuitable. To indicate the balance of E and B for an institution
we use the stochastic reference matrix $H_{SI}$ to relate an institution to already-classified sources. For each source~$s$, $H_{SI}[s,u]$ is the fraction of $s$'s reference weight directed at
institution~$u$, counted without regard to the total output of the source. 
The sum over sources, $f_{Fu}$, is the fraction of institution~$u$'s incoming reference weight from $F$-classified sources. These fractions are sorted and institutions assigned field labels by a quota rule: top 30\% by $f_{Xu}$ receive $X$;
from the remainder, top 10\% by $f_{Au}$ receive $A$; the rest are split $E$:$B$ at a 5:8 ratio reflecting the source ratio, with $E$ preferred when $f_{Eu}\geq f_{Bu}$. Classification of institutions by subfield is no more than tendency.
