\section{Corpus selection}
\label{sec:sources}

OpenAlex \citep{priemOpenAlexFullyopenIndex2022} provides our bibliometric data. Corpus selection starts with a list of eligible sources having three features: evidence from one of our journal registries that the source 
publishes economics or business research, an OpenAlex source identifier for corpus extraction, and a sufficient number of published works per year.

The journal registries are as follows. Harzing's \textit{71st Edition of the Journal Quality List}~\cite[][JQL]{harzingJournalQualityList2024} assembles
peer assessments of journal quality across business and economics
disciplines. Its scope is confined to economics and business so no
filtering is applied.
The \textit{ERA 2023 Submission Journal List}~\cite[][SJL]{australianresearchcouncilERA2023Submission2022} assigns up to three
Field of Research (FoR) codes to each entry. From the full list 
of 26,241 journals we retain those having all FoR codes falling within 
Division~35 (Commerce, Management, Tourism and
Services) or Division~38 (Economics). This applies to two-digit `division' codes and all four-digit `group' codes (3501--3509, 3801--3803, 3899).
\textit{Clarivate Web of Science Journal Citation Reports} ~\cite[][JCR]{clarivateanalyticsJournalCitationReports2026} assign each
journal a Web of Science category. We retain those in Economics, Management, Business, Transportation or Business, Finance.

Every journal in JQL, SJL, and JCR has at least one ISSN, and may have several. These registry ISSNs are joined independently to the February 2026 OpenAlex sources snapshot using its list of ISSNs as the matching key and selecting only the `journal' source-type. Journal titles are examined to identify around 30 additional matches where the OpenAlex source does not have an ISSN. The list of OpenAlex sources that are matched is designated OAS\textsuperscript{*}.

For OAS\textsuperscript{*} sources we extract work and authorship data to reveal some units that can be excluded because they publish very few works. To find a suitable threshold for excluding these units, we examine source and institution retention curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:retention}), selecting thresholds of $\tau_U = 20$ works/institution/year and $\tau_S = 20$ works/source/year. We designate the residual set of sources OAS.

Using units that satisfy these thresholds, we extract works, authorships and references with publication years from 2000 to 2024, retaining article and review types and excluding works that are paratext or retracted, or without an authorship entity. 

The sources cover the economics and business subfields within $\mathfrak{F}$, and it is of interest to compare spectral rankings for both. The three registries include one or more subfield indicators for each source, and OpenAlex sources have topic scores that indicate the relative intensity of business and economics subfields. Having categorised subfields as `E', `B' or 'None' (e.g. Psychology or Education), we classify sources as `E' when the summed number of indicators exceeds one `E' mention with no `B' mentions, `B' with the complementary definition, `A' when both subfields have more than one mention, and `X' for sources don't mention E or B. While sources are readily related to subfields, institutions generally publish substantial numbers of works in both subfields. Therefore, rather than use categorical classification, we classify institutions based on the ratio of their works in sources across the EBAX categories.
